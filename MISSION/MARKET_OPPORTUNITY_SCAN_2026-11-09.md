# Market Opportunity Scan — 2026-11-09

## Decision status
**No product is approved for launch yet.** The evidence supports demand for AI-assisted business content and operational guidance, but it does not prove willingness to buy this particular product, channel access, or conversion rates. A narrow pilot must validate those.

## 1. Primary demand signals

### Indian MSME technology adoption
A CII–ISB study reported that 65.8% of surveyed Indian MSMEs used AI tools, while 21% used ERP or customised business software. AI use was reported for business communication (34.9%), content creation (22.3%) and training (18.9%). The sample contained 2,919 confirmed MSMEs across states/UTs and sectors, but the report explicitly notes that participation was voluntary and not probability-weighted. Treat this as a directional signal, not a nationally representative market-size estimate.

Source: https://www.business-standard.com/amp/economy/news/two-thirds-of-msmes-use-ai-but-only-21-deploy-business-software-study-126101301191_1.html

### Small-business AI use and need for guidance
A Meta/YouGov study of 3,983 small-business owners across nine APAC markets (including India) reported 84% current AI use, 76% positive productivity impact, and 71% positive revenue/sales impact over the prior year. Among respondents whose businesses used AI, 51% used it to create/edit content, 43% for local advertising/marketing, and 37% for customer support/communications. 86% said small businesses need more guidance to get full benefits. The findings are aggregated across nine markets, not India-only, and are based on an online survey commissioned by Meta.

Source: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/10/more-than-three-quarters-of-small-business-owners-say-ai-makes-them-more-productive-but-most-need-more-guidance/

### Indian SME creative-content demand
An Amazon Ads/Opinium survey of 300 B2C marketing decision-makers at Indian SMEs found 88% likely to use AI to create advertising assets; 64% said they were already using AI for content generation. The survey was commissioned by Amazon Ads and is small relative to India's MSME population. It is a directional demand indicator, not proof of purchase intent for our offer.

Source: https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/small-business/ai-advertising-audience-growth-small-business

## 5. Recommended pilot hypothesis

Test a **business-specific, regional-language content pack**, not a generic AI content app.

Potential deliverable (scope to be tested):
- 12 ready-to-publish static/carousel concepts per month
- 4 short-video scripts/storyboards (not fake footage or recycled still-image videos)
- captions, CTA copy, WhatsApp enquiry text, and a 30-day publishing calendar
- business-specific offer messaging and local-language variants
- source/claim checks for factual or regulated claims
- client approval before any external publishing

Initial price test should not be set from competitor pages alone. The first experiment should test two willingness-to-pay levels with actual checkout intent, refunds tracked, and no fabricated testimonials or orders. Candidate test bands: ₹499–₹999 for a small self-serve starter pack, and ₹2,499–₹4,500 for a monthly content-only pack. These are hypotheses anchored to observed competitor price points, not recommended final prices.

## 6. Why this is only a pilot hypothesis
- Generic AI content tools are already cheap; "AI-generated posts" alone is not a moat.
- The current repository produces trend-led generic content, not yet a customer-specific brand pack.
- Existing video output relies on a repeated still image and is not yet a premium deliverable.
- No checkout, fulfilment, refunds, or settlement ledger exists.
- No primary interviews, paid pilot, preorder, or verified buyer conversion has occurred.
- A public price list is not proof of actual sales.

## 7. Required validation before launch
1. Select one vertical only after reviewing actual customer language and buying triggers.
2. Produce 3 sample packs from public business information or synthetic demo brands; do not scrape personal data.
3. Offer a preview and measure qualified checkout intent.
4. Secure at least one real paid pilot before building a full SaaS.
5. Verify payment-provider India onboarding, fees, refund handling, payout schedule and KYC before opening checkout.
6. Record revenue only after payment settlement.

The first operational milestone is a verified first settled customer payment, not a speculative revenue projection.

## 9. Current recommendation
Proceed to a narrow validation experiment for the content-only, regional-language pack. Do not build a broad SaaS, automate live publishing, or buy ads yet. The exact vertical and price must be chosen from customer evidence and a real paid pilot.
