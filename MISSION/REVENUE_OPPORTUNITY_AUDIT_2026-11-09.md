## Executive decision

Do not commit the mission to one product or one platform. Build a **portfolio of online, self-serve revenue experiments** around the fastest eligible settlement rails, while separately pursuing higher-ticket routes only when the buyer-discovery mechanism is real.

A payment page is not a customer-acquisition channel. Most creator storefronts provide checkout and delivery but explicitly expect the creator to bring traffic. The current account has no verified audience, customer base, live merchant account, approved marketplace listing, or payout history. Therefore, no platform discovered here can be treated as a ₹10 crore distribution engine by itself.

**The ₹10 crore / 30-day target remains the sole objective, but is an extreme stretch outcome, not a forecast or guarantee.** The present evidence does not support claiming that it is likely.

## 1. Evaluation criteria

Rank routes by:
1. Can an India-based individual/business become eligible and complete KYC promptly?
2. Does the platform provide genuine buyer discovery, or only checkout?
3. Can the offer be fulfilled without calls, meetings, manual demos, or owner-led service delivery?
4. How quickly do cleared earnings reach the Indian bank account?
5. Is zero-capital launch possible?
6. Can the channel produce meaningful transaction values and repeatable volume within the remaining 30-day window?
7. Are the platform terms, fees, holds, refund exposure and automation permissions compatible?

## 3. Critical finding: checkout is not distribution

The fastest payout platform is not necessarily the fastest route to money. A creator storefront can settle T+1 but still produce ₹0 if no qualified buyer reaches the page. Conversely, a marketplace may provide buyer intent but hold earnings beyond the deadline.

Therefore every revenue experiment must have two independent proofs:
- **Demand/discovery proof:** credible buyer search intent, platform search visibility, existing marketplace traffic, or measurable organic acquisition.
- **Settlement proof:** verified account eligibility, first-payout conditions, holds, fees, and bank arrival.

Do not mistake platform-wide creator payouts, marketplace GMV, user counts, or advertised prices for demand for our offer.

## 4. Chosen operating architecture

Run parallel, low-cost experiments, not a single-product bet:

1. **Fast-settlement self-serve storefront rail:** evaluate SuperProfile and Instamojo account onboarding. User handles only KYC/account authorization. The AI prepares product assets, descriptions, pages, delivery files and compliant publishing payloads. No live publishing or charge collection until the owner has approved the merchant account and required terms.
2. **Marketplace-discovery experiments:** investigate only platforms where a new seller can actually be discovered without bringing an existing audience, and where payout can reach the bank within the deadline. Gumroad Discover is not an early-stage acquisition shortcut because its threshold and risk-review gate are substantial.
3. **High-ticket digital/IP route:** investigate licensable software, data products, operational toolkits or packaged automation assets only when there is an existing buyer-intent channel and self-serve purchase/fulfilment is possible. No custom consulting that requires calls or manual delivery.
4. **Organic distribution automation:** publish useful, differentiated, rights-cleared assets through permitted search/social/community channels. No spam, fake engagement, mass unsolicited messages, scraping, or fabricated authority. Organic distribution is an experiment, not guaranteed traffic.
5. **Automatic fulfilment and reconciliation:** reuse the existing order core only after validating security and adding the actual approved provider integration. Do not expose paid access before server-side payment verification.

## 5. Day 1 execution sequence

### Gate 1 — Account/payout readiness (owner-only)
- User creates/opens the eligible account(s), enters identity/bank/tax details directly on provider sites, completes KYC, and authorizes required permissions.
- Never send passwords, OTPs, API secrets, full bank details, or identity documents in chat.
- Record only non-sensitive status: provider, KYC pending/approved, payout method verified, first-payout restrictions, exact published fees and settlement estimate.
- Do not open multiple accounts in violation of provider terms.

### Gate 2 — Asset and demand selection (AI-led)
- Build a scored opportunity shortlist using live marketplace search evidence, problem specificity, competitor quality, buyer intent, time to create, self-serve fulfilment and settlement feasibility.
- Select no more than 2–3 initial experiments; avoid spreading effort across dozens of weak ideas.
- Create only assets with clear buyer utility, original IP, testable quality and a direct purchase path.

### Gate 3 — Launch and instrument
- Prepare compliant product pages and demos.
- Publish only after required account-holder approval and provider authorization.
- Measure qualified views, checkout starts, captured orders, refunds, fees, payout eligibility, payout initiation and bank credits separately.
- Kill experiments with no meaningful buyer signal after a pre-set test window; redirect effort to the strongest signal.

## 6. Financial reality and decision guardrails

₹10 crore in 30 days from ₹0, without an existing audience or buyer pipeline, is an exceptionally low-probability outcome. No platform evidence reviewed establishes a credible path to that amount. The mission remains unchanged, but no revenue forecast should be invented.

A ₹10 crore bank credit would require, for example:
- 10 buyers paying ₹1 crore each; or
- 100 buyers paying ₹10 lakh each; or
- 1,000 buyers paying ₹1 lakh each; or
- 10,000 buyers paying ₹10,000 each.

The project currently has no verified evidence of any of these buyer cohorts. Therefore, treat the first captured and settled sale as a critical validation milestone, not as proof that the ₹10 crore target is on track.

No fake transactions, self-purchases, circular payments, borrowed funds presented as revenue, deceptive pre-sales, or unlawful financial activity.

## 9. EXPANDED REVENUE-PATH CENSUS — 2026-11-09

### Corrected strategic interpretation

The mission is **not a digital-product-selling project**. No revenue format is preselected. The decision problem is to find the fastest lawful mechanism that converts an economic value exchange into cleared bank cash by 2026-12-09, while requiring no routine owner-led sales or operations.

The strategy is therefore **route-first, transaction-first, and settlement-first**. Building a product is only one possible execution instrument after a route has passed demand, distribution, payout and automation tests.

### Evidence-based route filters

A route is disqualified from the primary plan if any hard gate fails:
1. It cannot legally be used by the relevant India-based person/entity.
2. First bank settlement is structurally after 2026-12-09.
3. It requires upfront working capital that is unavailable.
4. It depends on calls, meetings, visits, manual prospecting, manual sales or manual demos.
5. It has no credible buyer-discovery or demand mechanism.
6. It relies on fabricated transactions, policy evasion, spam, unauthorized access, IP infringement or unlicensed data.
7. It cannot fulfil or support the transaction lawfully and reliably.

### Route scorecard

Candidates that pass hard gates will be scored out of 100:
- 25 points — probability of cleared bank settlement by 2026-12-09.
- 20 points — real buyer demand and discovery without owner-led outreach.
- 20 points — realistic transaction value / gross contribution and volume required.
- 15 points — zero-capital launch and working-capital requirement.
- 10 points — degree of autonomous AI fulfilment and operations.
- 10 points — India eligibility, legal/IP/privacy/platform compliance and payout certainty.

A route with a high advertised prize, large TAM, high theoretical margin or impressive technology does not score highly unless it has an actionable path to a buyer and an actual bank payout within the deadline.

## 10. SECOND-PASS CHANNEL AND TRANSACTION MECHANISM AUDIT — 2026-11-09

### A. Online business / software acquisition marketplaces

**Acquire.com**
- Provides marketplace discovery and a high-ticket transaction mechanism for existing startups/businesses.
- Current published pricing: 8% closing fee below $250k, 7% from $250k to $1m, 6% above $1m; monthly listing fees $25/$50/$100 respectively.
- The seller process explicitly includes buyer evaluation, access requests, NDAs, LOIs, exclusivity, due diligence, legal review, asset purchase agreement and escrow. The platform itself recommends buyer conversations and warns against LOIs without live calls.
- Funds are released after buyer approves transferred assets during the inspection process.
- Decision: high transaction value but incompatible with the strict no-calls/no-manual-sales requirement and unlikely to complete a fresh listing, diligence and transfer inside 30 days without an existing revenue-generating asset and ready buyer. Do not select as primary under current known facts.
- Official sources:
  - https://help.acquire.com/what-is-the-acquisition-process-for-sellers
  - https://acquire.com/seller-pricing/
  - https://help.acquire.com/how-does-escrow-work-2

**Flippa**
- A real marketplace for websites, apps, domains and online businesses. Its current terms (updated 22 October 2026) confirm that sellers list assets and buyers contact/negotiate; Flippa does not itself buy or sell the asset.
- Listing fee is charged whether or not the asset sells. A buyer-interest guarantee can refund listing fees if no qualified buyer contact occurs within 30 days, but qualified contact may consist of NDA requests, proof-of-funds submissions, meetings or calls. That is not a guarantee of a completed transaction.
- Escrow.com releases funds only after transfer and buyer acceptance or expiry of inspection period; standard inspection periods are 7 days for websites/businesses, 3 days for apps and 2 days for domains. Identity verification and international bank/wire compatibility apply. International wire fee is stated as $20 for a seller bank outside the US in the Flippa escrow help article, in addition to relevant fees.
- Decision: has buyer discovery but a new sale is not self-serve or guaranteed; negotiation, asset ownership proof, legal agreement, transfer and inspection make 30-day settlement uncertain. Could be reconsidered only if the user has a verified transferable asset and agrees to the platform's transaction process; do not invent an asset or list without approval.
- Official sources:
  - https://flippa.com/terms-of-service/
  - https://support.flippa.com/hc/en-us/articles/202469614-Escrow
  - https://support.flippa.com/hc/en-us/articles/202470084-Payment-options

### B. API marketplace monetisation

**RapidAPI**
- Provider payouts occur after all charges for a month are consolidated and processed at the end of the following month. RapidAPI pays providers through PayPal only; the current provider fee page states a 25% marketplace fee for API Hub payments from 15 November 2025 (a legacy/versioned page still shows 20%, so the current 25% page is the controlling source for current planning).

- Official source: https://docs.rapidapi.com/docs/payouts-and-finance

**AWS Marketplace India**
- This is a more credible India-native software marketplace route than RapidAPI for payout configuration, but it is heavily gated.
- Seller registration requires a standalone AWS account, legal business identity, GSTIN, PAN, seller signature, GST e-invoicing enabled, matching legal name/address and Indian bank account. The public seller profile is stated to be approved in 1–3 business days, after which tax verification is required.
- India sellers can sell only to India buyers; public listings are priced in USD, private offers may be INR or USD; product types include SaaS, AMI, containers, professional services, ML and AWS Data Exchange.
- Seller can choose daily or monthly INR disbursement preference; however, the FAQ says disbursement timing aligns with standard AWS Marketplace disbursement practices. A daily preference is not proof that a new seller's first captured customer payment will reach the bank on the same day. Confirm account-specific timing and holds before relying on it.
- Decision: conditional candidate only if an eligible legal business with active GSTIN/e-invoicing and a relevant product already exists or can be built very quickly, and a buyer-discovery path can be demonstrated. Do not build a SaaS merely to enter AWS Marketplace. The requirement for GSTIN and India-only buyers sharply narrows the path.
